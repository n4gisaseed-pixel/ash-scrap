# World — 救われた後のセカイ

## Premise

Seven years after the hero defeated the Demon King, the world is still falling
apart. The victory ended the war, but the people who live afterward inherited
the ruins and the consequences of the route that won it.

The old world was held together by a magical circulation network. Five World
Cores regulated living systems: growth, water, wind and weather, heat and
geothermal pressure, and the central flow that connected them. The Demon King
used the Cores as a source of power. The hero's party destroyed four regional
Cores to weaken him, then defeated him at the central core. That was a rational
choice with the information they had. It also stopped the systems that kept the
world in balance.

The Cores are not simple batteries. They are closer to organs in a planetary
system. Repairing one does not instantly restore a forest, lake or climate; it
lets a damaged region begin recovering on its own timescale.

## The world seven years later

- Ash drifts through a dim sky. Some regions have no rain; others are trapped
  under constant storms.
- White, leafless forests and crystal growths replace living woods.
- Dried lakes expose the streets and rooftops of drowned cities.
- Magic storms and deep fissures divide the highlands.
- Rusted railways, abandoned inns, broken golems, war machines and old
  advertisements remain where people left them.
- Settlements survive by repairing old equipment, stripping ruins and adapting
  structures for new uses.

The world is a fantasy landscape with industrial ruins and magical machinery.
It should feel quiet and inhabited, with small pockets of warmth, food, jokes
and routine inside the desolation. The characters were born after the ending;
to them, ash and scrap are ordinary scenery.

## The five World Cores

1. **Green Core — Whitewood:** a dead forest where plants and beasts have
   crystalized around excess magic. Repair produces one tiny green shoot first.
2. **Water Core — The Dry Lake:** a city exposed on the lakebed. Its old pumps
   and waterways are now part of a scarce-water economy. Repair starts a trickle;
   the lake may take generations to return.
3. **Wind Core — The Broken Route:** a highland caught in a permanent magic
   storm, littered with fallen airships. Repair opens a safe passage through the
   mountains.
4. **Fire Core — The Furnace City:** an industrial district kept warm by
   equipment that consumes its own remaining parts. Ash must give the system a
   sustainable job instead of merely shutting it off.
5. **Crown Core — The Old Demon Castle:** the central junction that links the
   four regional systems. Its records reveal why the hero destroyed the other
   Cores and why the Demon King exploited them.

## Starting region

### Ash's workshop and the scrap settlement

An improvised settlement built around old heat pipes and a repair economy. Ash
has never seen the world before the war. Mina runs a repair stall and helps
explain what the people here need from the old machinery.

### The scrapyard and old Demon Army facility

Scrap is both material and history. An old patrol machine still follows a war
order that no longer has a purpose. Azami is following a hand-updated map toward
the facility when she is pinned beneath a shifting wreck.

### The local furnace relay

This is not a World Core. It is an auxiliary station in the old network. Repairing
its pressure system restores a faint signal to Azami's map and briefly sends
water through an abandoned public cistern. That small result proves her story is
possible and points toward Whitewood.

## Character/world relationship

### Ash

Ash is a 15-year-old junk dealer and mechanic. He fixes weapons, household
objects, magical tools and machines with parts he finds in ruins. He does not
remember the old world and has no nostalgia for it. His question is what a thing
can do now, not what it used to be.

### Azami

Azami is a bright, blue-clad demon wanderer and cartographer. Her hand-drawn map
changes as she discovers that roads, lakes and settlements no longer match old
records. She initially knows the World Cores by instinct, not by explanation.
As the central junction reconnects, ancient systems identify her as a possible
successor to the Demon King. This is a dangerous system label, not proof that
she wants to rule or harm anyone.

### The old hero

The hero who defeated the Demon King is not a villain. The party acted to stop a
war and made the best decision their information allowed. Their old records
should let players understand the cost without turning victory into a simple
mistake.

## Narrative rules

- The people who inherited the world are not waiting to be rescued; they have
  built lives and systems that work for them.
- Repairs should create practical, visible improvements before they create
  spectacle.
- The heroes of the previous RPG were neither stupid nor malicious. They lacked
  the missing context about the Cores.
- Machines and ruins carry old purposes, but their new purpose is chosen by the
  people who use them.
- The final conflict is about preventing history from repeating, not killing a
  newly designated Demon King.

## Central questions

- What should be preserved when restoring the old world would harm the people
  who live in its ruins now?
- Who gets to decide that a place, a machine or a person is beyond repair?
